Investment Philosophy

The Stewardship Thesis

We back for-profit companies built, from day one, to share ownership widely. Not out of charity — because it's the more durable way to build.

01

The Leverage Shift

What AI Actually Changes

AI doesn't just automate tasks; it automates judgment. A handful of people can now do what once took thousands. When leverage gets that cheap, the constraint stops being capacity — and starts being intent.

02

The Fork in the Road

Concentrated vs. Distributed

That leverage can pool wealth in very few hands, or spread it across many. The technology doesn't decide which — the ownership we design into companies does. Left on default, it concentrates.

03

Stewardship as Strategy

Why Distribution Is Sturdier

Shared ownership isn't only fairer, it's harder to break. Companies whose value is widely held resist capture, keep their people, and tend to outlast the founders who started them.

Concentrate or Distribute

How We Partner

The Practice of Stewardship

Money is the easy part. The harder work is structural — how ownership gets split, how decisions get made, and how the upside compounds for everyone who builds it. That's where we spend our time.

01

Patient Capital

We hold for decades, not exit windows. Good companies compound — we'd rather not interrupt them to manufacture a return.

02

Ownership by Design

Broad ownership works when it's built in at incorporation, not bolted on at exit. We help set it up right the first time.

03

Governance as Infrastructure

We help wire decision rights into how a company runs — so resilience is structural, not left to good intentions.

04

Conviction over Consensus

We're comfortable being early and alone, as long as the reasoning holds and the people are serious.
